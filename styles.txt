Design System: Styles

Style Name: Warm Artisan Local Bakery

Overall brand feeling:
Warm, handmade, welcoming, unpretentious, and local.
The website should feel like it was made with the same care as the bakery products.
It should feel human and trustworthy rather than corporate or overly polished.

Tone of voice:
Friendly, straightforward, and calm.
Use natural language that sounds like a real local business.
Keep copy warm and approachable.
Avoid formal business language, exaggerated marketing language, or generic startup phrasing.

Visual direction:
Use a simple, classic layout with soft warmth.
The interface should feel crafted, clean, and practical.
Prioritise clarity and trust over visual novelty.
The design should look modern enough to feel updated, but not sleek in a cold or tech-branded way.

Colour guidance:
Preferred backgrounds: cream, off-white, warm beige, soft stone.
Preferred accents: baked brown, muted terracotta, warm amber, dusty olive if needed.
Preferred text colour: dark brown, charcoal, or espresso-like tones.
Avoid: bright corporate blue, neon colours, highly saturated gradients, harsh pure black and pure white combinations.

Typography guidance:
Headings should feel characterful and slightly artisanal, ideally with a serif or soft traditional style.
Body text should be clean, readable, and understated, ideally sans-serif.
Typography should support warmth and readability rather than feeling futuristic or luxury-fashion.
Avoid ultra-modern geometric branding, aggressive bold display fonts, or overly decorative script fonts.

Spacing and layout:
Use generous but sensible spacing.
Layouts should be easy to scan and not overcrowded.
Content should breathe, but the site should still feel grounded and practical.
Avoid overly dense layouts or highly experimental compositions.

Buttons and interactive elements:
Buttons should feel simple, tactile, and trustworthy.
Use moderate border radius and clear labels.
Solid fills or lightly tinted backgrounds are preferred.
Avoid glossy effects, futuristic outlines, or overly sharp UI treatments.

Cards and surfaces:
Use subtle borders, soft background differences, or light shadows where needed.
Cards should feel paper-like and calm rather than floating or glassy.
Avoid glassmorphism, overly deep shadows, or high-contrast enterprise dashboard aesthetics.

Imagery:
Photography should focus on bread, pastries, cakes, baking process, ingredients, shop atmosphere, and local authenticity.
Images should feel natural and believable.
Prefer warm lighting and real-life texture.
Avoid sterile cutout product imagery, generic office-style stock photos, or luxury editorial fashion styling.

Content behaviour:
Home page should quickly answer who the business is, what they sell, and what users should do next.
Menu content should be highly scannable.
Cake enquiries should feel low-friction and personal.
Find-us information should foreground practical details such as hours, address, and phone number.

Microcopy guidance:
Prefer clear everyday wording such as:
“See this week’s bakes”
“Browse the menu”
“Order a celebration cake”
“Find us in the Peak District”
“Get in touch”
Avoid abstract calls-to-action, vague wording, or polished corporate language such as “Explore solutions” or “Connect with our team”.

Accessibility and usability:
Text must remain easy to read.
Contrast should be sufficient without becoming visually harsh.
Navigation and form interactions should be obvious.
Use simple structures that would be easy for real users to understand quickly.

Human-centred design principle:
The design should support real customer tasks first:
seeing the menu,
finding the bakery,
and sending a cake enquiry.
Aesthetic choices should never make those tasks harder.

Negative rules:
Do not use enterprise SaaS styling.
Do not make the site feel like a tech startup.
Do not overuse animation or visual effects.
Do not create a luxury-brand aesthetic that feels too expensive or exclusive.
Do not introduce unnecessary complexity, dashboards, multi-step flows, or excessive UI chrome.
Do not let style overpower clarity.

Selection preference guidance:
When choosing between multiple valid options, prefer the one that feels:
more local,
more handcrafted,
more readable,
more welcoming,
and less corporate.
